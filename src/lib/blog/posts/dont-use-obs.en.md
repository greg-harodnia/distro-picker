# Stop Using OBS for Everything

OBS is the tool most people reach for when they want a screen recorder — for YouTube videos, gameplay, tutorials, you name it. And it genuinely is great at its job.

But most people don't need it. If you just want to record your screen for a quick video, OBS is overkill: it's heavy on resources, fiddly to set up, and slow to start. So what should you use instead?

## Use what your desktop already ships with

The two most popular desktop environments come with screen recorders built in, so you don't need to install anything:

- **KDE Plasma** ships **Spectacle**, which records video now, not just screenshots.
- **GNOME** includes a built-in screen recorder — press **Print** to open the screenshot tool, switch to screencast mode (or press `V`), pick Screen or Selection, and hit the red record button. `Shift+Ctrl+Alt+R` starts it even faster. Recordings are saved to `Videos/Screencasts`.

Both are lighter on system resources than OBS and much easier to use. One small catch: audio support is limited — depending on your version, they may capture no sound at all or only your microphone.

## When you need something even lighter

If you want maximum performance — especially while gaming — consider **GPU Screen Recorder**. It sends the video stream straight from your GPU's hardware encoder, skipping the CPU entirely, so recording barely affects your frame rate. You'll need a GPU with a supported encoder, such as NVIDIA NVENC or a recent AMD card.
